How It Works

After you choose an image, the browser decodes it and draws it onto an invisible canvas. If you set a maximum size, the drawing is scaled down to that size. The canvas is then saved again in the output format you picked, using the quality you chose for JPEG and WebP. The new file is shown with its size compared to the original, and you can download it when you are happy with the result. No step sends your image to a server.

Why Compress Images?

Faster websites

Smaller images download faster, which helps pages load quickly on slow connections and mobile data.

Easier uploads

Many forms, portals and email services limit file size. A smaller copy is easier to attach or submit.

Choose what you trade off

Pick a quality level, a maximum size and a format, and see the size change before you download.

100% private

Your photos stay on your device. The image is never sent to a server, so personal pictures stay private.

Choosing the Right Settings

For photos, JPEG at a quality between 70 and 85 is a common starting point. It often reduces the file a lot while the picture still looks sharp on screen. For screenshots, text and logos, PNG is usually better because it is lossless, but it will not shrink much when re-saved. WebP gives smaller files than JPEG at similar quality and keeps transparent areas. If your image is much larger than the space you need, reducing the maximum size often saves more space than lowering the quality.

Final Thoughts

Compressing an image is most useful when you need a smaller file for a website, a form or an email, and you can accept a small loss of detail. Check the preview and the size change before you download. If the picture needs to stay exactly as it is, keep the original file.
